# GST 111: Research Skills and Library Resource Management

## Module 1: Study Skills and Methods
**Definition of Study**: Studying is the application of oneself to the acquisition of knowledge. It involves an active engagement with material to fully comprehend and retain it.

**Effective Reading Techniques**:
- **Skimming**: Glancing through material to get a general overview without paying close attention to details.
- **Scanning**: Selectively reading to locate specific information, such as keywords or technical terms.
- **Intensive Reading**: Thorough and time-consuming reading used for examinations to comprehend and reproduce details.
- **Extensive Reading**: Reading for pleasure to gain general knowledge.

**Study Methods**:
- **SQ3R Method**: Survey (previewing), Question (asking preliminary questions), Read (thorough reading), Recall (mental rehearsal), Review (checking goals).
- **KWL Method**: Know (what you already know), Want (what you want to know), Learn (what you have learned).

**Note-Taking vs. Note-Making**: Note-taking happens during lectures at the lecturer's pace, while note-making is a personal activity involving revisiting and revising notes to fill gaps and correct errors.

**Time Management**: Successful students must prioritize and use a timetable or workload plan. Goals for each session should be SMART (Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Realistic, Time-bound).

## Module 2: Library Resources and Organization
**The Library**: Defined as an organized information system concerned with collection, processing, storage, and dissemination of information for reading and research.

**Categories of Libraries**:
- **Academic**: Tertiary institutions (e.g., Kenneth Dike Library)
- **Special**: Caters to specific classes of people (e.g., IITA library)
- **Public**: General libraries for all people, often called "the people's university"
- **National**: The apex library and major archive of a nation

**Library Resources**: Printed Resources (books, journals, maps) and Non-printed/Electronic Resources (microform, CD-ROM, E-databases)

**Reference Materials**: Intended for consultation of specific items rather than consecutive reading. Examples: Encyclopaedias, Dictionaries, Atlases, Gazetteers, Biographical Dictionaries.

**Classification Schemes**:
- **Library of Congress (LC)**: Alphanumeric notation (A=General Works, Q=Science, K=Law)
- **Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC)**: Numeric notation (000=Computer Science, 500=Science)
- **Moys Classification Scheme**: Specifically for legal information resources

**ICT in Libraries**: OPAC (Online Public Access Catalogue) for searching library holdings electronically.

## Module 3: English Grammar and Usage
**Morphemes**: The smallest meaningful unit of grammar.
- **Free Morphemes**: Can stand alone (play, test)
- **Bound Morphemes**: Must attach to another morpheme (-ful, -ed)
- **Inflectional Morphemes**: Grammatical functions like plurality (-s) or tense (-ed) without changing word class
- **Derivational Morphemes**: Create new words or change category (teach → teacher)

**Word Classes**:
- **Open Classes**: Nouns, Verbs, Adjectives, Adverbs
- **Closed Classes**: Pronouns, Prepositions, Conjunctions, Determiners

**Sentence Structure**:
- **Simple**: One independent clause
- **Compound**: Two+ independent clauses joined by coordinating conjunctions
- **Complex**: One independent + at least one dependent clause
- **Compound-Complex**: Combines both compound and complex elements

## Module 4: Research and Information Literacy
**Information Literacy**: Ability to identify an information need, locate sources, evaluate them for authority and accuracy, and use them effectively.

**Intellectual Property and Copyright**: Laws protecting intellectual works.

**Referencing Styles**:
- **APA 7th Edition (2020)**: Parenthetical in-text citations (Author, Year)
- **MLA 9th Edition (2021)**: Used in humanities and languages

## Module 5: Writing and Summary
**The Writing Process**: Choosing a Subject → Delimiting the Subject → Deciding on Audience/Purpose → Generating an Outline → Actual Writing → Proofreading

**Forms of Writing**:
- **Correspondence**: Official (memos, formal letters) or Unofficial
- **Essays**: Narrative, Descriptive, Expository, Argumentative

**Summary Writing**: Reducing a passage to its main ideas in one's own words. Rules: avoid fragments, avoid lifting (copying word-for-word), maintain brevity.
